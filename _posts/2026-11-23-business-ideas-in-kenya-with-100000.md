**What business can I start with 100k in Kenya?** KSh100,000 can launch a small cleaning service, a focused mitumba or cosmetics operation, phone-accessories resale, food pre-orders, laundry pickup, a local supply route or certain skill-based services. A basic one-chair barber setup may also fit in a shared space.

The catch is working capital. A shop that spends KSh100,000 on rent, fittings and stock has nothing left for the second month.

This guide compares **10 businesses to start with KSh100,000 in Kenya**, with a complete KSh100,000 sample budget for each. The figures are **November 2026 planning estimates, not supplier quotations or promised profits**. They assume modest equipment, limited opening stock, an owner-operated launch and no major construction or vehicle purchase.

## How to divide KSh100,000 before you open

The following is a simple default budget, not a rule for every business:

| Use | Planning amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment or opening stock | KSh45,000 |
| Registration, permits and setup allowance | KSh10,000 |
| Marketing, packaging and first deliveries | KSh10,000 |
| **Cash working-capital reserve** | **KSh35,000** |
| **Total** | **KSh100,000** |

Your reserve pays for restocking, fuel, data, repairs and operating costs while customers become regulars. It is **not** the value of unsold stock. If you are renting premises, aim to hold at least two months of fixed expenses after paying the deposit and opening bill. If your quotations do not fit, start smaller or choose another model.

## 1. Home and office cleaning business

A mobile cleaning business is one of the more practical **small business ideas in Kenya with 100k** because you can begin without a rented shop. Your customers might be apartment owners, small offices, landlords preparing homes for new tenants or short-stay accommodation operators.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Cleaning tools, buckets and basic machine hire allowance | 28,000 |
| Chemicals and protective gear | 12,000 |
| Transport, client visits and marketing | 8,000 |
| Setup, records and compliance allowance | 7,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **45,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Start with one service, such as move-in or deep cleaning, and build a price sheet after seeing the property. Charge separately for unusual stain removal, high windows or extra travel. Do not promise a sofa or carpet machine if you only have enough money to hire one per job.

**Test before launching:** ask three estate managers or landlords how often they book cleaners, what they pay and what makes them change providers. One repeat contract is worth more than a large number of unconfirmed enquiries.

**Main risk:** damage claims and poor-quality work. Use written job checklists, protective equipment and clear customer sign-off.

## 2. Curated mitumba and second-hand fashion

Mitumba has fast turnover *when you know the customer*. It is much harder when you buy an unfamiliar sealed bale and hope the right buyers appear.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Hand-picked opening stock or shared bale | 45,000 |
| Cleaning, photos, hangers and packaging | 8,000 |
| Market days and transport | 7,000 |
| Selling, records and setup allowance | 5,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **35,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Choose one niche: office wear, children's clothes, denim, sportswear or plus-size clothing. Photograph actual pieces, show sizes and defects, and list them on WhatsApp Status, TikTok, Instagram or at market days.

**Test before launching:** sell 20–30 selected items. Record the buying price, cleaning cost, selling price, unsold pieces, returns and delivery charges. Only then increase stock.

**Main risk:** dead stock. Reduce prices on styles that do not move; do not keep buying new bales to hide old losses.

## 3. Cosmetics and everyday personal-care products

Cosmetics resale can work with KSh100,000 **if you start with a small, repeat-purchase range**. A fully stocked beauty shop in a prime location is a different and more expensive business.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Authentic opening stock | 45,000 |
| Shared-shelf display, labels and packaging | 8,000 |
| Delivery, content and customer acquisition | 5,000 |
| Records, compliance and setup allowance | 7,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **35,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Begin with everyday products customers reorder, rather than tying up capital in many premium brands or shades. Partnering with an established salon or barber can cost less than taking your own lease.

**Test before launching:** list ten products and compare three traceable suppliers. Ask real customers which three items they run out of most often and how much they pay locally.

**Main risk:** counterfeit and slow-moving stock. Keep supplier invoices, avoid suspiciously cheap products and check relevant product safety requirements.

## 4. Phone-accessories resale

Phone cases, charging cables, screen protectors and other accessories can be sold online, through local delivery or from a **shared** retail counter. The KSh100,000 plan is not for a full phone shop.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Selected opening stock | 45,000 |
| Display, demonstrations, basic security | 10,000 |
| Delivery and launch marketing | 5,000 |
| Records and setup allowance | 5,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **35,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Stock what fits the phones people around you actually use. Avoid ordering large quantities for unpopular models. Test cable and charger quality, ask suppliers about returns, and explain warranties accurately.

**Test before launching:** ask repair shops which accessories sell weekly. Start with a small range and restock only proven items.

**Main risk:** changing phone models, returns and theft. A high markup on an item that sits unsold for six months is not a profit.

## 5. Food pre-orders and small-event catering

Food can generate repeat customers, but a full takeaway shop or restaurant brings rent, equipment and compliance costs that may exceed KSh100,000. A simpler model prepares meals **against confirmed orders**, using an existing suitable kitchen.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Missing utensils, cookware and safe storage | 25,000 |
| Initial ingredients, containers and packaging | 20,000 |
| County health and compliance allowance | 10,000 |
| Selling, transport and delivery | 10,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **35,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Serve one customer group: office lunch orders, pre-booked meetings, workers at a nearby site or small family functions. A limited menu controls buying and food waste. Collect a deposit for event orders and calculate cost per plate, including energy, packaging and transport.

**Test before launching:** pre-sell a small batch on two different days. Record actual food costs and leftovers before increasing orders.

**Main risk:** food safety, spoilage and underpricing. This model is viable only where the kitchen and handling arrangements meet the applicable county and public-health requirements.

## 6. Laundry pickup and ironing service

KSh100,000 is not a budget for a commercial laundry fitted with multiple machines. It may be enough for a **pickup, washing coordination and ironing service** in a compact estate, especially if you already have dependable washing facilities.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Irons, boards, baskets and garment tags | 20,000 |
| Detergents, hangers and packaging | 8,000 |
| Pickup route, launch materials and customer acquisition | 10,000 |
| Records and setup allowance | 7,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **55,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Offer fixed pickup and delivery days. Tag each item and record condition before accepting it. Price by garment or load, and charge extra for express service or distant deliveries.

**Test before launching:** recruit five households or short-stay hosts for a paid trial. Measure collection time, processing costs, water and power use, losses and delivery cost per customer.

**Main risk:** damage and lost garments. A route with too few clients can also consume the margin in transport.

## 7. Supplying fast-moving goods to local SMEs

If you know kiosks, salons, workshops or small offices that buy the same goods weekly, a micro-distribution route can work without a warehouse or delivery vehicle. Think **small quantities of shelf-stable consumables**, not a full FMCG wholesale business.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Opening stock for a few confirmed buyers | 55,000 |
| Hired transport and handling | 8,000 |
| Stock records and communication | 2,000 |
| Setup and sales visits | 5,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **30,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

Choose one short delivery route and five to ten products. Confirm quantities and prices before buying. Focus on repeat orders and prompt payment. Source more expensive items against customer orders where practical.

**Test before launching:** get at least five shops to name the products they buy weekly, their current supplier prices and the terms they expect. Make a small, cash-on-delivery test run.

**Main risk:** thin margins and credit. A KSh20,000 invoice paid next month cannot buy stock this week. Track margins *after* delivery and breakages.

## 8. A small poultry cycle using existing facilities

Poultry is an option only if you already have appropriate space, housing, water, time and a buyer plan. **This is not enough capital to acquire land or build a complete farm from scratch.**

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Basic house upgrades, brooding and feeders | 20,000 |
| Chicks from a reputable supplier | 18,000 |
| Feed, litter and veterinary allowance | 23,000 |
| Hygiene, biosecurity and transport | 9,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **30,000** |

The number of birds this budget supports depends on breed, purchase price, feed requirements and the production cycle. Do not fix a bird count before obtaining current prices and a feeding plan. Ask a local veterinary professional about vaccination and disease prevention.

**Test before launching:** price the full feed requirement, estimate expected mortality conservatively and identify buyers willing to pay a realistic farm-gate price. Plan for delayed sales.

**Main risk:** disease, rising feed costs and weak selling prices. Poultry cash flow is tied to production cycles, not guaranteed daily earnings.

## 10. One-chair barber service in a shared space

A very basic barber service can fit KSh100,000 when rent is modest, premises already have power and adequate hygiene facilities, and the owner cuts hair. It is **not** the budget for a high-end or multi-chair barbershop.

**Sample KSh100,000 budget**

| Item | KSh |
|---|---:|
| Chair, clippers, mirrors and sanitation tools | 30,000 |
| Shared-space fee or modest deposit and first rent | 18,000 |
| Towels, capes and consumables | 8,000 |
| Permits, signs and launch marketing allowance | 9,000 |
| **Cash reserve** | **35,000** |
| **Total** | **100,000** |

A barber earns from repeat visits. A clean workspace, consistent service, opening hours and customer retention matter more than expensive décor. If the owner cannot cut hair, paying a barber from the start changes the economics considerably.

**Test before launching:** count existing barbers within walking distance, their prices and the number of chairs in use at peak and off-peak times. Get an actual quote for the shared space before buying equipment.

**Main risk:** poor foot traffic and rent. If the location requires major plumbing, electrical work or an expensive deposit, reject the KSh100,000 plan.

## Which is the most profitable business to start with 100k in Kenya?

The useful comparison is **profit after real expenses and payment for your work**, not an online claim about daily sales.

| Your advantage | Businesses worth testing first | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A marketable skill, phone or laptop | Social media services, cleaning | Small inventory commitment |
| Existing customers who buy every week | FMCG supply, personal-care products | Repeat purchases |
| Product knowledge and online audience | Mitumba, phone accessories | Ability to select and sell winning stock |
| Approved kitchen, repeat food orders | Food pre-orders | Customers can order before production |
| Barbering skill and a low-rent space | One-chair barber service | Repeat local visits |
| Suitable existing farm facilities and buyers | Poultry pilot | Avoids expensive new premises |

**Example: Mitumba profit is not the same as sales.** Suppose you sell 80 pieces in a month at an average KSh850 each:

| Illustration | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sales: 80 × KSh850 | KSh68,000 |
| Cost of pieces sold: 80 × KSh500 | −KSh40,000 |
| Packaging and deliveries | −KSh5,000 |
| Market fees, data and other operating costs | −KSh8,000 |
| **Surplus before tax and paying the owner** | **KSh15,000** |

This is only an **illustration of the calculation**, not an earnings forecast. Unsold items, refunds, weaker prices and extra travel can make the result lower or negative.

Use the same formula for every model:

**Monthly business surplus = sales − cost of goods or services − operating expenses.**

Then separately account for applicable taxes, loan repayments, asset replacement and fair compensation for the owner's time. A business that keeps you working 250 hours a month for KSh15,000 pays very little for your labour.

## How to start a business in Kenya with KSh100,000: a seven-day test

1. **Day 1 — Choose one customer group.** An estate, a group of salons or a cluster of offices is easier to serve than “everyone in Kenya.”
2. **Day 2 — Ask about actual purchases.** Speak to at least ten potential customers. Ask what they buy, how often, from whom and at what price.
3. **Day 3 — Check competitors.** Visit them when business is busy and when it is slow. Look for gaps in reliability, delivery or stock.
4. **Day 4 — Get three supplier quotations.** Include delivery fees, taxes, equipment condition, warranty and minimum order size.
5. **Day 5 — Verify permits.** Check county rules, public-health needs, product requirements and the correct registration structure before taking premises.
6. **Day 6 — Make a paid test sale.** A paid order or deposit is stronger evidence than someone saying they like the idea.
7. **Day 7 — Write a go/no-go budget.** Keep working capital separate. If the business only fits by removing the reserve, it does not fit.

For an existing KSh50,000 business, an extra KSh50,000 should usually expand **proven orders or stock**, not automatically buy a bigger shop. Compare the [KSh50,000 ideas](/blog/business-ideas-in-kenya-with-50000/) before spending more.

## Registration, county permits and tax basics

The [Business Registration Service](https://brs.go.ke/fee-schedule-companies-registry/) lists **KSh950** for registering a business name. That is a registration charge, **not** the total price of legally opening a shop. Company registration is a different process and cost.

Depending on the business and county, you may need a county business permit, health approvals, food-handler certification or other activity-specific licences. Use the [Kenya Trade Portal county licence listings](https://www.kenyatradeportal.go.ke/counties/licenses/p/field_county_value/All/page/faq) and your county's official licensing office to verify fees for the exact location before signing a lease.

KRA requires that businesses onboard eTIMS and issue electronic tax invoices, subject to its published procedures. Check your actual income-tax, turnover-tax and VAT obligations with the Kenya Revenue Authority. Tax thresholds and rates can change; the amount of startup capital alone does not determine what taxes apply.

Keep a daily record of sales, paid invoices, supplier purchases, returns, expenses and stock. Mixing household spending with restocking money is one of the fastest ways to lose track of profit.

## Businesses that generally do not fit a KSh100,000 budget

These may be attractive businesses, but do not force them into a KSh100,000 headline:

- **Full mini-supermarket:** shelves, broad stock, refrigeration, security and rent can exhaust the amount.
- **Independent M-PESA outlet:** float, security, provider terms and premises must be funded; confirm requirements first.
- **New car wash site:** water, drainage, pumps, access and a lease can cost more than the available capital.
- **Fully equipped salon or commercial laundry:** machines, fitting, consumables, staffing and operating cash add up.
- **Large poultry farm:** housing, feed, biosecurity and cash tied up until sale demand more investment.

## Final verdict

If you have **KSh100,000 to start a business in Kenya**, choose the smallest version that can attract paying customers and still leave money in the account. For someone without a shop, cleaning, online resale and skill-based services are logical first tests. For someone with customers or facilities already in place, a supply route, food pre-orders, barbering or a small poultry cycle may be worth examining.

The best business is not the one that spends the entire KSh100,000. It is the one that **proves demand, controls costs and survives long enough to build repeat sales**.
