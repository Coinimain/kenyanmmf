KSh1 million is serious business capital, it is also easy to lose.

Rent takes the first bite, rquipment follows, and stock takes another. The doors then open with a bright signboard and no money for wages, electricity, transport or restocking.

This guide compares **15 businesses to start with KSh1 million or more in Kenya**. It covers retail, services, distribution, manufacturing and energy.

For a wider list across several capital bands, read our guide to [25 business ideas in Kenya](/blog/business-ideas-in-kenya/). If your budget is much smaller, use the separate guide to [15 businesses to start with KSh50,000 in Kenya](/blog/business-ideas-in-kenya-with-50000/).

## Best businesses to start with KSh1 million at a glance

KSh1 million fully funds a narrow service or equipment-hire operation.

It partly funds a stock-heavy shop.

It does not fully fund a modern factory, petrol station or large processing plant.

| Available capital | Practical shortlist | Capital priority |
|---:|---|---|
| KSh1 million–KSh2 million | Event equipment, commercial laundry, specialised printing, compact minimarket | Working capital and a narrow launch model |
| KSh2 million–KSh5 million | Hardware, FMCG distribution, gym, tiles, timber, authorised LPG retail | Stock depth, transport and three months of operations |
| KSh5 million–KSh15 million | Bottled water, maize milling, nail manufacturing | Plant use, quality control and distribution |
| Above KSh15 million | Non-woven bags, larger processing plants, petrol station | Technical design, approvals, infrastructure and long cash cycles |

Do not choose the biggest operation your money enters, choose the smallest complete operation that serves paying customers.

## How to divide a KSh1 million business budget

This starting split suits a lean retail or service business:

| Use of funds | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Equipment or opening stock | KSh450,000 |
| Premises deposit and fit-out | KSh150,000 |
| Registration, permits, insurance and safety | KSh50,000 |
| Transport, selling and launch marketing | KSh50,000 |
| Working capital | KSh300,000 |
| **Total** | **KSh1 million** |

The KSh300,000 reserve is not idle cash.

It pays rent, wages, utilities, transport and suppliers while sales build. It also covers the gap between delivering an order and receiving payment.

Equipment cannot pay a worker, an unpaid invoice cannot restock a shelf.

Factories, LPG outlets and petrol stations need technical budgets. A percentage split is not enough for them.

## Businesses to start with KSh1 million to KSh2 million

### 1. Tents, chairs and event equipment

Event hire earns from the same assets many times.

The first package must match confirmed demand. Start with popular chair types, common tent sizes, tables and basic linen. Add décor, public-address systems and specialist equipment after customers request them regularly.

A lean KSh1 million operation needs room for storage, transport, loaders, cleaning and repairs. A KSh2 million to KSh4 million operation carries more seating capacity and handles overlapping events.

Asset use decides the return.

Record the number of paid hire days for every item. Record transport, damage, loss and repair costs. Take a deposit. Use signed dispatch and return sheets.

Buying 500 chairs is easy, keeping them booked is the business.

### 2. Commercial laundry

A commercial laundry sells reliable handling and convenience.

The KSh1.2 million to KSh4 million range covers commercial washers, dryers or finishing equipment, water storage, plumbing, electrical work, tagging supplies, premises and working capital. A collection vehicle raises the budget.

Start with one dense route. Apartment blocks, short-stay rentals, salons, restaurants, clinics and small hotels provide repeat loads. Scattered household pickups burn fuel and time.

Machine use controls profit. An expensive washer running two hours a day is trapped capital.

Tag every item. Record stains and damage at intake. Price water, power, detergent, labour, packaging, collection and delivery into each kilogram or item.

### 3. Printing and branding workshop

Printing starts cheaply as a brokerage service. Owning production equipment changes the budget.

A KSh1.5 million to KSh7 million workshop covers combinations of digital printing, large-format work, garment branding, binding and finishing. New, used and imported machines carry different costs and risks.

Do not buy every machine at launch.

Own the equipment used every week. Outsource rare jobs. Price design, material, setup, machine time, finishing, delivery and reprint risk.

Collect a deposit. Get written approval for spelling, dates, colours, sizes and quantities. One error across 2,000 items wipes out several good orders.

Keep cash for toner, ink, print heads, maintenance, power backup and spare parts.

### 4. Compact mini-supermarket

A mini-supermarket is a stock-turnover business.

The money goes into shelves, refrigeration, a point-of-sale system, security, deposits, signage, opening stock and working capital. KSh1 million only supports a compact minimarket in a controlled location. A stronger opening starts at about KSh1.5 million.

Fast-moving goods deserve the shelf, slow stock pays no rent.

Track gross margin by product category. Milk, bread, flour, toiletries, drinks and household goods carry different margins and expiry risks. Reconcile cash, mobile payments and stock every day.

Ban casual withdrawals from the till. Set rules for staff purchases, damaged goods, supplier returns and customer credit.

### 5. LPG cylinder retail outlet

LPG retail is a regulated energy business.

The KSh1.2 million to KSh4 million range fits a compliant cylinder outlet operating through an authorised brand relationship. It covers the cylinder cage, scales, fire protection, premises, opening stock, delivery arrangements and working capital.

EPRA's [LPG cylinder retail requirements](https://www.epra.go.ke/sites/default/files/2024-12/Licence%20for%20Retail%20of%20LPG%20in%20Cylinders.pdf) require proof of ownership of at least 5,000 cylinders or written distributorship authority from a licensed LPG cylinder brand owner. County, tax, fire, weighing-scale and premises documents also form part of the process.

A new retailer needs an authorised relationship. Random cylinder trading creates legal and safety exposure. Secure site and licensing guidance before signing a long lease. Reject premises that fail access, ventilation, spacing and fire-safety requirements.

## Businesses to start with KSh2 million to KSh8 million

### 6. Hardware and building-materials shop

Hardware needs depth in the items customers request every day.

The core range includes fasteners, plumbing parts, electrical items, tools and paint accessories. Supply bulky or expensive materials against confirmed orders until demand proves the need for permanent stock.

A KSh1.5 million to KSh6 million budget covers racks, security, rent, opening stock, delivery arrangements and working capital. The site must sit near active construction, dense housing or a reliable contractor network.

Credit destroys weak hardware shops. Set limits, and put delivery and payment terms in writing. An unpaid contractor invoice is not usable cash.

The KNBS [2026 Economic Survey](https://www.knbs.or.ke/reports/2026-economic-survey/) reported 6.8% growth in construction during 2025. The sector grew, but a poor location and weak stock controls still lose money.

### 7. FMCG distribution

Distribution turns small margins into profit through volume and route discipline.

The KSh1.5 million to KSh6 million range covers opening stock, a store, delivery transport, staff, route selling and working capital. A formal distributorship with supplier targets costs more than a small owner-operated route.

Start with a short product list, and build one profitable route. Then add the next. Measure margin after fuel, loading, returns, breakages, commissions and bad debts.

Cash collection is the control point. Sales figures mean little when retailers pay late.

Set written credit limits, and stop supply when the limit is breached. Stock must turn back into cash before the supplier invoice falls due.

### 8. Commercial gym

A commercial gym combines equipment, space and recurring memberships.

The KSh2.5 million to KSh8 million range covers commercial strength and cardio equipment, flooring, mirrors, changing facilities, ventilation, sound, security, rent and working capital.

Home-use machines fail under heavy commercial use.

Pre-sell founding memberships before finishing the fit-out. Count households, offices, schools and competing gyms inside the true catchment area.

Track active members, sign-ups, renewals, visits and revenue per member. January sales look impressive. Renewal rates reveal the business.

Group classes, personal training and corporate packages increase revenue without filling the floor with more machines.

### 9. Tiles and sanitaryware shop

Tiles carry high value in limited space. They also bring weight, breakage and design risk.

The KSh2 million to KSh7 million range covers displays, rent, stock, handling, delivery and working capital. Sanitaryware, grout, adhesives and fittings increase the average sale.

Show enough variety to win trust. Do not fill the store with slow colours and discontinued patterns.

Record every batch and shade. A customer returning for extra boxes needs a close match. Log damaged cartons and broken pieces immediately.

Homeowners, fundis, contractors and developers buy differently. Prepare quotations for each group. Take deposits for special orders.

### 10. Timber yard

A timber yard serves contractors, furniture makers, carpenters and repair work.

The KSh2.5 million to KSh8 million range covers a secure yard, sheds, opening stock, cutting and planing equipment, power, handling, security and working capital.

Stock the sizes and species the local market buys.

Too little variety sends customers away. Too much slow stock locks up cash and deteriorates. Measure sales by size, species and customer group.

Buy from traceable suppliers. Keep purchase and transport records. Confirm current forestry, county and movement requirements before ordering.

Security is compulsory. Timber is valuable, bulky and difficult to reconcile after a loss.

### 11. Bottled-water processing

Bottled water is a manufacturing and distribution business.

The project needs a lawful water source, treatment equipment, tanks, filling and sealing equipment, packaging, testing, premises, quality controls and delivery. A compliant small operation starts around KSh3 million. More automation pushes the cost above KSh10 million.

The machine is one line in the budget.

Bottles, caps, labels, shrink film, transport and retailer margins repeat in every production run. Packaging consumes large amounts of cash.

Manufacturers must follow the applicable [KEBS Standardization Mark process](https://www.kebs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/FAQs-Quality-Assurance.pdf). Factory inspection, product testing and quality records belong in the launch plan.

Secure routes before buying a large line. Water in a warehouse produces no return.

## Manufacturing businesses above KSh5 million

### 12. Sifted maize-flour mill

A maize mill earns through disciplined grain buying, efficient production and strong distribution.

The KSh6 million to KSh25 million range covers milling and sifting equipment, weighing, packing, electrical work, premises, grain, packaging, staff and working capital. Capacity and automation create the wide range.

Maize prices hit the business first.

Bad grain buying cannot be repaired by attractive packaging. Model yield, by-products, electricity, labour, fortification, packaging and distributor margins for every tonne.

Food manufacturing needs strict quality controls. Confirm public-health, KEBS, NEMA, workplace and county requirements before ordering equipment.

Do not buy a ten-tonne line without a ten-tonne market. Distributor commitments must support the planned output.

### 13. Nail manufacturing

Nail manufacturing converts steel wire into standard construction products.

The KSh5 million to KSh15 million range covers nail-making machines, polishing and sharpening equipment, electrical installation, raw materials, packaging, quality control, staff and distribution.

Machine speed is not profit. Saleable output is the business.

Calculate steel-wire cost, power, rejects, downtime, packaging and delivery per kilogram. Compare that result with the wholesale selling price.

A plant operating below planned capacity spreads rent, payroll and equipment costs across too few kilograms. Build hardware, wholesaler and contractor routes before expanding output.

### 14. Non-woven bag manufacturing

Non-woven bag manufacturing sits far above the KSh1 million entry point.

A complete operation needs raw-material supply, cutting, sealing, punching, printing, power, factory space, operators, packaging and distribution. An integrated line costs KSh15 million to KSh50 million or more.

Imported equipment and inputs add exchange-rate, shipping and spare-parts risk.

Before ordering a line, secure written specifications, power requirements, output rates, installation, training, warranty and local maintenance support.

Capacity must follow contracts. Supermarkets, manufacturers, retailers and event companies order different sizes, thicknesses and print runs.

A factory must know its customer before installation.

### 15. Independent petrol station

A petrol station is a property, engineering, compliance and working-capital project.

The KSh25 million to KSh80 million range excludes expensive land. Tanks, civil works, canopy, piping, dispensers, drainage, fire systems, power, water, security, initial fuel stock and operating cash drive the budget.

The site comes first.

Traffic counts alone are not enough. Access, turning space, road approvals, competition, customer mix and future road changes decide the location.

EPRA's [petroleum retail licensing requirements](https://epra.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-02/Licence%20for%20Retail%20of%20Petroleum%20Products%20%28Except%20LPG%29.pdf) cover facility ownership or lease documents, environmental approval, county and planning approvals, fire clearance, workplace registration, tank and dispenser calibration, pressure tests and an emergency response plan.

Do not buy land or begin construction from a generic petrol-station estimate. Complete site, traffic, engineering, environmental and financial studies first.

## Which high-capital business is most profitable?

There is no universal winner.

The cash model decides the result.

| Business model | Main strength | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Retail | Daily sales and visible demand | Thin margins, theft and dead stock |
| Distribution | Repeat orders and route scale | Credit risk and transport costs |
| Equipment-led service | The same asset earns repeatedly | Idle capacity and maintenance |
| Manufacturing | Product control and wholesale volume | High fixed costs and distribution pressure |
| Fuel and LPG | Essential products and frequent sales | Heavy safety, site and compliance demands |

Retail produces quicker cash.

Manufacturing offers scale.

Neither guarantees profit.

The strongest business converts capital back into cash, protects its margin and keeps fixed costs below realistic sales.

## Test the business before spending the money

High capital does not replace market research.

Complete these checks first:

1. Name the paying customer.
2. Count direct competitors within the trading area.
3. Record real prices, order sizes and buying frequency.
4. Get three supplier quotations.
5. Confirm the premises and utility requirements.
6. List every licence and approval needed before opening.
7. Test one route, one product range or one customer segment.
8. Collect deposits, letters of intent or trial orders where possible.

Compliments do not prove demand, orders do.

## Why a KSh1 million business needs a written plan

A high-capital business plan is not a school essay.

It is a decision document.

It answers five hard questions:

1. **Who pays?** Name the customer, location, order size and buying frequency.
2. **What does the capital buy?** List equipment, stock, fit-out, compliance and reserves.
3. **When does cash return?** Show the time between paying suppliers and receiving customer money.
4. **What can stop the launch?** Identify approvals, utilities, skills and supplier delays.
5. **What result justifies the risk?** Calculate break-even, owner surplus, debt service and investor return.

A generic sample business plan does not answer those questions for a named town and budget.

A useful business plan shows every assumption behind the figures. It changes when the rent, machine quote, selling price or launch date changes.

## What a complete Kenyan business plan must contain

A full plan includes:

- Executive summary
- Ownership and business model
- Customer and competitor research
- Location analysis
- Products, services and pricing
- Supplier and equipment schedule
- Licences, taxes, insurance and compliance
- Staffing and daily operations
- Sales and marketing plan
- Itemised startup budget
- Monthly operating costs
- 12-month cash-flow forecast
- Profit-and-loss forecast
- Break-even analysis
- Funding request and use of funds
- Risk controls
- Launch schedule

The numbers must connect.

The sales forecast drives stock, staffing and cash flow. The equipment list matches production capacity. The funding request matches the startup budget.

An attractive cover does not repair broken figures.

## Registration, taxes and licences in Kenya

The [Business Registration Service fee schedule](https://brs.go.ke/fee-schedule-companies-registry/) lists KSh950 for a business name and KSh10,650 for a private limited company. High-capital projects often use a company structure to define ownership, shares and contracts. The right structure depends on the owners, activity and financing.

Registration is only the first layer.

County permits depend on the activity and location. Factories and higher-risk projects also face environmental, workplace, fire, planning, product-quality and sector approvals.

NEMA tells project proponents to obtain the required environmental approval before implementation. DOSHS handles workplace registration, inspection and occupational safety.

Tax compliance starts on day one. Read our [KRA 2026 tax guide for small businesses](/blog/kra-2026-tax-regime-survival-guide/) for the wider rules and risks.

The core thresholds are:

- [Turnover tax](https://www.kra.go.ke/individual/filing-paying/types-of-taxes/turnover-tax-tot) is 1.5% of gross sales for qualifying resident businesses with annual turnover above KSh1 million and up to KSh25 million.
- Compulsory [VAT registration](https://www.kra.go.ke/individual/filing-paying/types-of-taxes/value-added-tax) starts when annual taxable supplies reach KSh5 million.
- People carrying on business must onboard eTIMS and issue compliant electronic invoices.

Our detailed [eTIMS guide for Kenyan businesses](/kra-etims-merchant-tax-compliance-certificate-expense-validation-2026/) explains invoice validation, expense records and the 2026 compliance changes.

Turnover tax excludes certain income, including management, professional and training fees. Other income-tax rules also apply in some cases. Confirm the correct treatment with KRA or a qualified tax professional.

## Calculate the break-even point

Use numbers. Not hope.

**Monthly break-even sales = monthly fixed costs ÷ gross-margin percentage**

Assume a hardware shop has fixed monthly costs of KSh180,000. Its average gross margin is 18%.

The break-even sales figure is KSh1 million a month:

**KSh180,000 ÷ 0.18 = KSh1,000,000**

That comes before loan principal, owner drawings and income tax.

If the shop trades for 26 days, it needs average daily sales of about KSh38,462 to cover those fixed costs.

Test a weak month. Test a normal month. Test a strong month.

Then test what happens when stock rises in price or customers pay late.

## Compare the business with passive investments

Capital has an opportunity cost.

Before locking the money into stock, machinery or a lease, study the options in our [beginner guide to investing in Kenya](/blog/complete-beginner-guide-to-investing-in-kenya/).

For money that can remain invested for longer, compare the project with [Kenya government bonds](/blog/kenya-government-bonds-treasury-bonds-guide/) and the wider range of [Special Funds in Kenya](/blog/special-funds-in-kenya/).

Compare projected after-tax investment income with the business's expected owner surplus after every cost. Put a value on your own time.

The business must offer enough upside to pay for work, illiquidity and loss risk.

Do not put emergency money into machines, stock or a long lease. Keep household reserves outside the project budget.

If the capital is coming from outside the country, the guide on [how to invest in Kenya from abroad](/blog/how-to-invest-in-kenya-from-abroad/) covers account setup, transfers, tax records and due diligence.

## Final verdict

KSh1 million opens the door to a serious business, it does not remove risk.

Start with customers. Build the full budget, and protect working capital. Confirm approvals before paying for premises or equipment.

Manufacturing and energy projects need technical studies before deposits are paid. The idea starts the conversation, the plan decides whether the money survives.
