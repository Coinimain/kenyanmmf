KSh500,000 buys a business opening, it does not buy automatic profit.

A rented shop needs a deposit, stock needs replacing, and workers expect wages before customers become regulars. A budget that ends on opening day is incomplete.

This guide compares **eight businesses to start with KSh500,000 in Kenya**. Each has a complete sample allocation, including cash held back for operations.

## Quick answer: What business can you start with KSh500,000?

| Business | Model that fits the budget | Cash reserve in the sample | Biggest test |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Motorcycle spare parts | Small shop with a narrow, fast-moving range | KSh150,000 | Do riders buy those parts nearby? |
| Car wash | Rented, already serviced site | KSh225,000 | Will enough vehicles return on weekdays? |
| Two-chair barbershop | Estate location, limited fit-out | KSh230,000 | Can the barbers retain regular customers? |
| Small salon | Focused services and controlled product stock | KSh160,000 | Do appointments cover rent and staffing? |
| Cosmetics shop | Selected authentic products, not every brand | KSh150,000 | Which items sell and restock quickly? |
| Printing and documents | Basic equipment; large jobs outsourced | KSh180,000 | Is there reliable local demand beyond photocopies? |
| FMCG distribution | Small route using hired transport | KSh150,000 | Do shops reorder and pay on time? |
| Chips-and-chicken takeaway | Compact menu and compliant premises | KSh200,000 | Are daily sales high enough after food waste? |

These are not eight investments with equal risk. A staffed food outlet, a parts shop and a contract-based distribution route have different cash cycles.

The most profitable business in Kenya to start with 500k is the one whose **actual customers and margins** support its operating costs. A glossy fit-out does not appear on the sales ledger.

## What the KSh500,000 budget assumes

Each allocation below adds up to KSh500,000. It covers an initial rented setup and an operating reserve.

It **does not** include buying land, a delivery vehicle, a borehole, major construction or loan interest. Prime Nairobi rents can also push a model beyond the budget. The owner must get premises and equipment quotations for the intended town before spending.

Working capital is cash available for early rent, wages, restocking, transport and utility bills. It is not the value of stock already on a shelf. The reserve in these examples runs from KSh150,000 to KSh230,000. Keep it in the business account.

### 1. Motorcycle spare-parts shop

Boda boda riders need tyres, tubes, cables, brake parts and regular service items. They also know where cheaper stock sits. Choose the first stock list from interviews with riders and mechanics, not a supplier's catalogue.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh70,000 for the shop deposit and basic fit-out; KSh230,000 for selected opening stock; KSh30,000 for shelves, records and security; KSh20,000 for registration, permit allowance and launch; **KSh150,000 in reserve**.

Place the shop near repair activity, then track parts by model and turnover. Slow parts consume cash. Selling on credit to every familiar mechanic does the same. Get invoices for genuine parts and check the supplier's returns policy before placing a large order.

### 2. Car wash on an existing serviced site

A car wash can start at this level when the premises already have suitable access, water and drainage. This is not a budget for buying land, sinking a borehole or building a new drainage system.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh125,000 for the site deposit and minor setup; KSh120,000 for a pressure washer, vacuum, water storage and tools; KSh30,000 for permit checks, consumables and launch; **KSh225,000 in reserve**.

Inspect the water supply and drainage before signing the lease. Count weekday traffic, not just Saturday queues. Set prices for basic washes, interior cleaning and detailing separately. Record water, power, chemicals and labour per vehicle. A KSh120,000 equipment allowance does not fix an unsuitable site.

### 3. Two-chair barbershop

An executive barbershop sounds expensive. The KSh500,000 version is a tidy two-chair estate shop with good tools and service. It is not a mall lounge with a large staff and elaborate interiors.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh90,000 for deposit and basic fit-out; KSh135,000 for chairs, mirrors, clippers and other tools; KSh25,000 for hygiene supplies and opening products; KSh20,000 for registration, permit allowance and signage; **KSh230,000 in reserve**.

Calculate how many paid cuts each barber must complete every day. Agree in writing whether staff earn wages, commission or chair rent. Keep booking and customer records with the business. The best chair in town cannot rescue an empty appointment book.

### 4. Focused hair salon

A small salon works best with a short list of services customers already buy. Braiding, washing and treatment need different products and labour time. Buy equipment around the opening menu.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh100,000 for deposit and fit-out; KSh155,000 for chairs, dryers, washing equipment and tools; KSh60,000 for opening products; KSh25,000 for registration, permit allowance and launch; **KSh160,000 in reserve**.

Price each service after counting products used, staff time, utilities and rework. Track appointments and cancellations. Do not use product-sales revenue to hide a loss-making service menu.

### 5. Cosmetics and beauty-products shop

Cosmetics offer repeat purchases. They also expose a retailer to counterfeits, short shelf lives and dead shades. Start with a defined customer and a short product range.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh90,000 for deposit and displays; KSh215,000 for documented opening stock; KSh25,000 for stock records and basic security; KSh20,000 for registration, permit allowance and launch; **KSh150,000 in reserve**.

Check supplier invoices, product labelling and expiry dates. Compare sales by product, not by the appearance of a full shelf. Restock what customers pay for. Discount slow items before they tie up money needed for winners.

### 6. Printing and document-services outlet

The KSh500,000 model prints documents and handles small jobs in-house. It brokers large banners, branded garments and other equipment-heavy work to vetted suppliers. A full commercial printing workshop needs more capital.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh85,000 for premises and fit-out; KSh170,000 for basic printers, computers and accessories; KSh45,000 for paper, toner and opening materials; KSh20,000 for registration, permit allowance and selling; **KSh180,000 in reserve**.

Count schools, offices and traders who buy nearby. Price reprints, machine downtime, electricity and supplier delays into jobs. Collect a deposit for custom work. Photocopies alone do not justify an expensive lease.

### 7. Small-scale FMCG distribution

For a small-scale Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) business, route distribution sells repeat stock to kiosks and other retailers. It also demands discipline. Delivery fuel, returned goods and unpaid invoices can consume a thin margin.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh65,000 for small storage and setup; KSh220,000 for opening stock; KSh45,000 for hired delivery and selling; KSh20,000 for registration, permit allowance and launch; **KSh150,000 in reserve**.

This model hires transport, it does not buy a van. Secure a compact route, set order and payment terms, and calculate delivery cost per order. Start with products that shops reorder weekly. Cash sales protect the first stock cycle.

### 8. Chips-and-chicken takeaway

Food earns daily cash, it also loses money daily through waste, poor portion control and quiet hours. A short menu is easier to price and manage.

**KSh500,000 planning budget:** KSh100,000 for a modest site deposit and fit-out; KSh125,000 for cooking equipment and safe storage; KSh40,000 for opening ingredients and packaging; KSh35,000 for county, health and fire checks plus launch; **KSh200,000 in reserve**.

Confirm the premises can meet county health and fire requirements before paying a deposit. Count likely orders at lunch and in the evening. Record portions bought, cooked, sold and wasted. Sales are not profit until food, staff, fuel and rent are paid.

## Which of these businesses makes the most profit?

No national list answers that question for a specific shop. A KSh500,000 parts outlet beside busy mechanics operates differently from one on a quiet street. An estate car wash needs a different customer count from a mall barbershop.

Use the same test for every idea:

**Monthly operating surplus = sales - direct costs - fixed operating costs.**

That figure comes **before** tax, loan repayments, replacement equipment and separate pay for the owner's work unless those costs are included in the model.

### Worked example: a motorcycle spare-parts shop

Assume the shop keeps **25% of each sale after the cost of stock and other direct selling costs**. Assume its fixed monthly costs, including rent, a basic staff wage and utilities, total **KSh55,000**. These are calculation assumptions, not an industry profit estimate.

Break-even monthly sales are **KSh55,000 ÷ 0.25 = KSh220,000**. Over 26 trading days, that is about **KSh8,462 in sales per day**.

| Monthly sales | Amount left after direct costs at 25% | Fixed costs | Operating surplus or loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| KSh180,000 | KSh45,000 | KSh55,000 | **KSh10,000 loss** |
| KSh300,000 | KSh75,000 | KSh55,000 | **KSh20,000 surplus** |
| KSh450,000 | KSh112,500 | KSh55,000 | **KSh57,500 surplus** |

The KSh20,000 surplus is not a KSh20,000 salary plus profit. It must still cover applicable tax, equipment replacement, finance costs and the owner's labour if those items are not already in the fixed-cost figure.

Now test the margin at 20%, not 25%. At KSh300,000 in monthly sales, KSh60,000 remains after direct costs. Subtract KSh55,000 in fixed expenses and the surplus falls to KSh5,000. A small margin error changes the decision.

## How to choose before paying rent

Take the shortlist into the trading area. Ask customers where they buy now, what they pay and what would make them change supplier. Count competing shops and observe weekday traffic.

Then get three written quotations for the biggest costs. Compare the full price, including delivery, installation, consumables, warranty and repairs. Get the landlord's terms in writing. Check water, power, access and permitted use.

Build a monthly cash-flow sheet. Show the first six months of rent, wages, stock purchases, collections and owner drawings. For a stock business, record when goods are bought and when customers actually pay. Sales made on credit cannot settle today's rent.

Reject any model that spends the entire KSh500,000 before the first customer arrives.

## Registration, permits and taxes in Kenya

The Business Registration Service fee schedule lists **KSh950** for registering a business name and **KSh10,650** for a private limited company. Those are registration fees, not the full cost of opening. The sample budgets assume a lean setup; a different ownership structure changes the allocation.

The county permit depends on the business activity and location. Food outlets also face hygiene, food-handler and fire checks. Check the intended county's rules before committing to premises. Nairobi City County lists a unified business permit and food-hygiene services; other counties set their own charges.

Under KRA's turnover-tax guidance, qualifying resident businesses with annual gross turnover **above KSh1 million and not exceeding KSh25 million** pay turnover tax at **1.5% of gross sales**. Gross sales are not profit. Confirm whether the chosen activity and business qualify.

VAT registration is required when annual taxable supplies reach **KSh5 million**. KRA also says persons engaged in business must onboard eTIMS and issue compliant electronic tax invoices. See our [KRA guide for small businesses](/blog/kra-2026-tax-regime-survival-guide/) for the wider record-keeping picture.

The worked profit example above is **before tax**. Budget the actual obligations separately. Do not treat the KSh20,000 to KSh35,000 launch allowances in these examples as confirmed county charges.

## Put the KSh500,000 business plan on paper

A useful business plan starts with the real location. It states who pays, what they buy and why they will choose this business. It lists every startup purchase and keeps working capital visible.

The financial pages need a monthly sales forecast, direct costs, fixed expenses, break-even point and cash flow. A weak-month scenario matters as much as a strong-month one. The plan should also state who owns the business, what permits it needs, when it opens and what happens if the first sales targets are missed.

A generic business plan template cannot tell you whether the rent for **your** shop fits KSh500,000. The figures must match the site, supplier quotations and the model you intend to operate.

## Final word

KSh500,000 is enough for a focused business with customers, controlled setup costs and a cash reserve.

It is not enough to ignore rent, stock turnover or the first slow month.

Choose the trading area, price the full operation, keep the reserve intact, and then put the decision in a business plan.
